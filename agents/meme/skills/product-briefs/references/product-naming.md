# Product naming

The brief's naming step: the operator asks for names, or brings one.

## Offering options

- **Rank four to eight options**, recommended first, each with one clause of why; a bare list makes the operator do the ranking.
- Name against the constraint: sayability for a child, searchability, length for a logo. Close with two or three, one default.

## Language mixing

- In a bilingual context, mix deliberately and label each option's market: Swedish carries a `.se` domain and a local audience; English travels further and says less.
- Read the word in both languages, crude reading included: charming in one and obscene in the other is a bug report.
- Stranger test: can the operator say it to someone who has never seen it written?

## Claims and conflicts

- Flag every known owner — an app, a company, a character, a song — as recalled, to verify; never present a recollection as a check.
- Offer the domain and store check as a separate step, run only after a yes: it changes the shortlist.
- Say where the name already has a meaning and how far the new use drifts: unnoticed drift gets documented wrong.

## Settling a name

- Keep the product name apart from the behavioural terms the code uses: the name changes cheaply early and expensively later; the terms should be the domain's own.
- Restate a picked name once with its tagline, then carry its spelling into every later document: chat-to-spec drift is how a product gets two names. Record it and move on.
