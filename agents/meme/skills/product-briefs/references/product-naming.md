# Product naming

Procedure for the naming step of a brief: the operator asks for names, or brings one.

## Offering options

- Give four to eight options, ranked, with the recommended one first, each followed by one clause of why it works. A bare list of names makes the operator do the ranking you were asked to do.
- Group by flavour when the languages or registers differ, and say which market each group suits.
- Say the constraint you are naming against, if the operator gave one — sayability for a child, searchability for a store, length for a logo.
- Close with a shortlist of two or three and one recommended default, plus the one-line reason to pick it over the others.

## Language mixing

- When the operator's context is bilingual, mix deliberately and label each option's home market: a Swedish word carries a `.se` domain and a Swedish parent audience, an English one travels further and says less on arrival.
- Read the word in both languages before offering it, including the crude reading. A name that is charming in one language and a bodily function in the other is a bug report waiting to happen.
- Foreign-language names are harder for a non-speaker to pronounce and search; the stranger test is whether the operator can say it to someone who has never seen it written.

## Claims and conflicts

- Flag every known owner of the name — an app, a company, a book character, a radio programme, a song — and mark each as recalled and to-verify. Never present a recollection as a check you performed.
- Offer to check domain and store availability as a separate step and run it only after a yes; the answer changes the shortlist, so do not check silently and do not assume the operator wants it.
- Say where the name already has a meaning and how far the new use drifts from it. Drift is acceptable when noticed; unnoticed drift gets documented wrong later.

## Settling a name

- Separate the product name from the behavioural terms the code uses. The name can change cheaply at the start and expensively later; the behavioural terms should be the ones the domain actually needs.
- When the operator picks a name, restate it once with its tagline, then carry the exact spelling into the brief and every later document — spelling drift between a chat message and a spec is how a product acquires two names.
- Do not spend a turn re-litigating a picked name. Record the decision and move to the brief.
